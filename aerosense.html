AeroSense · SIH 2026

Catch what
breath carries.

Most drugs don't evaporate. They leave the lungs inside microscopic droplets of airway fluid. AeroSense is a handheld reader designed to catch those droplets in a pre-cast hydrogel pad, read them electrochemically, and give a Reference, Abnormal or Inconclusive result in under a minute, fully offline. If someone can't or won't blow, the same pad takes a fingertip instead.

The science

Why we catch droplets instead of sniffing gas.

Alcohol is volatile, so it evaporates into breath as a gas and a sensor can "smell" it. Drugs like THC, opioids and amphetamines are heavy, non-volatile molecules. They don't become gas. They leave the body a different way.

No gas

Drugs aren't volatile

THC, opioids and amphetamines are heavy molecules. Unlike alcohol, they don't evaporate into breath, so a gas sensor can't smell them.

0.5–5µm

They ride out in droplets

The fluid lining the deep airways exchanges compounds with the blood, then breaks into microscopic droplets with every breath.

0needles

A sample without a needle

Catch those droplets and you have a non-invasive sample of what's circulating. Prof. Olof Beck's research has shown drugs are present in this aerosol after use.

The hydrogel pad

A wet circuit, waiting for a sample.

A thin gel is cast onto the electrode strip at the factory and sealed in foil. It keeps the strip ready to read, so the officer never adds a liquid and the sensor never starts dry.

~1%agarose

A mesh that holds water

A loose polymer mesh keeps the liquid in place. The gel is about 99% water, so a droplet that lands on it simply becomes part of it.

0.9%saline

Salt to carry the current

Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions move charge between the working, reference and counter electrodes, closing the circuit the moment the sample arrives.

Redoxprobe

A probe that trades electrons

Ferri/ferrocyanide mixed into the gel hands electrons to the carbon during the sweep: [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ → [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ + e⁻. That flow is the current we read.

Dual mode · Touch mode

No breath? A fingertip works too.

At the roadside, some people refuse to blow, fake a blow, or stop early. Instead of ending the test, AeroSense switches to the same hydrogel pad, this time read through a fingertip. One cartridge, two ways in.

Breath fails the gate

Under 1.5 L, or no breath at all. The screen asks for Touch mode.

Cover slides open

A sliding cover on the cartridge front exposes the gel pad.

Hold 5–10 s

A capacitive ring around the pad checks the finger stays put. Lift early and it's Inconclusive.

Same sweep

Same gel, same carbon, same DPV reading as Breath mode.

The same pad, under a fingertip

The cover slides off, the finger holds for the timer, and sweat from the pores soaks into the gel. From there it's the same read as a breath.

Inside a fingertip

Fingertips have the highest density of sweat glands on the body, and sweat carries what's circulating in the blood.

①blood → gland

From the blood to the sweat gland

Capillaries wrap around each eccrine gland, a coiled tube deep in the dermis. Drugs in the blood pass into it by passive diffusion.

400+/cm²

Up a spiral duct to a pore

Sweat carries them up a duct that spirals through the outer skin and opens on the top of a fingerprint ridge, the part pressed against the gel.

③into the gel

Even when the skin feels dry

Glands release tiny amounts of sweat all the time. The salty gel draws it in on contact, with no pumps or liquid pouches.

④same read

The same carbon, the same sweep

The molecules drift down to the electrode and block it just like breath droplets do, so the result is read the same way.

01

Capacitive gate

Skin and the ring electrode form a capacitor. The ESP32's touch pin sees the jump, and the sweep only starts once contact is held.

02

Single-use pad

Skin oil and dead cells would foul the carbon, so every pad is used once and thrown away.

03

Wipe first

A quick wipe before the touch removes residue picked up from handles or cash, so the test reads what's in the sweat, not on the skin.

04

Subtract the background

Two working electrodes share the gel. WE1 reads the background, WE2 reads background plus target, and the firmware uses ΔI = I(WE2) − I(WE1).

Anatomy

Take it apart, one layer at a time.

Keep scrolling. The model follows along.

01 · the reader

One hand, one breath.

A palm-sized reader with a screen and three result lights. Everything that touches the sample lives in the cartridge on top.

02 · the cartridge

Single-use, zero carryover.

Mouthpiece, Venturi nozzle, and an electrode strip with its pre-cast hydrogel pad come as one sealed cartridge. It clicks in, and it's thrown away after one test.

03 · the capture

Droplets down, gas out.

Breath turns sharply above the strip. Droplets keep going and land on the electrode; gas escapes through the side vents. The droplets merge straight into the gel.

04 · the electronics

A lab bench on one board.

A flow sensor checks the breath, a potentiostat reads the strip, and an ESP32 runs the classifier. An 18650 cell powers a full shift.

05 · the fallback

No breath? Use the pad.

The cover on the cartridge front slides aside to expose the gel. A capacitive ring around it waits for a fingertip to rest there for 5–10 seconds.

06 · the answer

A result an officer can act on.

The curve is compared against a reference on the device itself, and one of three lights comes on. No lab, no network.

What's new

The science exists. Pocket-sized, it doesn't yet.

Breath-aerosol collectors already exist for research, but samples are sealed and mailed to a lab for mass spectrometry. Companies like Hound Labs have shown breath-based THC testing is commercially viable, with premium hardware, and fingerprint sweat tests exist too, read optically over about ten minutes. Our aim is to bring capture and analysis together in one low-cost handheld device that takes either breath or a fingertip.

01

Impaction, not filtration

Easy to blow through, and the droplets collect exactly where the sensor is.

02

Flow-verified sampling

No result is given until the breath volume shows a deep-lung sample.

03

Zero carryover

Everything that touches the sample is thrown away, so the reader never fouls.

04

Offline edge AI

The whole analysis runs on the device, so it works anywhere.

05

Dual mode

Breath or fingertip, gated by a flow sensor and a capacitive ring. A refusal to blow no longer ends the test.

06

Pre-cast hydrogel

A solid-state wet pad replaces pumps, blisters and liquid pouches. No moving parts, no dry-sensor failures.

07

Seconds, not minutes

An electrochemical sweep reads in seconds, where optical sweat strips take around ten minutes to develop.

08

Two working electrodes

A control electrode reads the background so humidity and skin oil cancel out of the result.

Demo plan

Proving it safely, without a single controlled substance.

Testing with real narcotics isn't legal or safe outside authorised forensic labs under the NDPS Act. So our demo proves the full physical and electrochemical pipeline using safe surrogates.

01

Breath-sized mist

A medical mesh nebulizer makes a 1–5 µm saline aerosol, the same size range as exhaled breath aerosol.

02

Visible impaction

A dye tracer in the mist shows the droplets landing on the electrode while air leaves through the exhaust.

03

Measurable drop

A clean saline mist and one carrying a bulky, harmless blocker (such as gelatin) give clearly different peaks, and the device classifies each one live, by breath or by touch.

our answer to "did you test real drugs?"

For real drugs, the capture, measurement and classification stay exactly the same. Only the receptor on the electrode changes, developed and validated with an authorised lab partner.

What the prototype will prove: aerosol capture, the electrochemical readout, and on-device classification of the signal. What comes later: drug-specific detection limits, which need validated receptors and controlled clinical trials.
